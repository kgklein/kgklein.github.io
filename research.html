Research

My research investigates the physical processes governing energy transfer and particle energization in weakly collisional plasmas. The solar wind provides an extraordinary natural laboratory for this work, allowing spacecraft observations to be compared directly with predictions from plasma theory and numerical simulations.

Our work combines analytical theory, numerical modeling, and observations from missions including Parker Solar Probe, Solar Orbiter, and Wind. Three interconnected areas form the foundation of this research.

Turbulence and plasma heating

How does turbulent energy move across scales, and which physical processes ultimately transfer that energy to particles?

We study turbulent cascades, intermittency, kinetic-scale fluctuations, and the connections between spatial structure and collisionless energy transfer. A central objective is to identify the mechanisms responsible for plasma heating and determine how their relative importance changes across different plasma environments.

Highlighted publications

Evidence for electron Landau damping in space plasma turbulence
Chen, Klein & Howes (2019), Nature Communications.

Using spacecraft measurements of electromagnetic fields and electron velocity distributions, we identified a velocity-space signature consistent with electron Landau damping in turbulent plasma. This demonstrates how field–particle correlations can directly constrain collisionless energy-transfer mechanisms in space plasmas.

Mind the gap: non-local cascades and preferential heating in high-beta Alfvénic turbulence
Gorman & Klein (2024), Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society: Letters.

We investigate how nonlocal interactions in Alfvénic turbulence influence the transfer of energy across scales and its redistribution between particle species. This work connects the properties of turbulent energy cascades to preferential heating in high-beta plasma environments.

Kinetic plasma physics

Space plasmas often depart strongly from local thermodynamic equilibrium, exhibiting temperature anisotropies, relative drifts, and non-Maxwellian particle velocity distributions.

We investigate how these departures influence wave propagation, kinetic instabilities, and wave–particle energy exchange. Numerical dispersion tools and spacecraft measurements provide complementary constraints on the stability and evolution of collisionless plasmas.

Highlighted publications

Majority of Solar Wind Intervals Support Ion-Driven Instabilities**
Klein et al. (2018), Physical Review Letters.

By applying linear kinetic stability analysis to measured solar-wind ion distributions, we demonstrated that a majority of sampled intervals support ion-driven instabilities. This work established the importance of considering the combined effects of multiple ion populations, temperature anisotropies, and differential flows when assessing plasma stability.

The dielectric response of plasmas with arbitrary gyrotropic velocity distributions
Klein & Verscharen (2025), Physics of Plasmas.

We develop methods for evaluating the kinetic dielectric response of plasmas with arbitrary gyrotropic velocity distributions, including the treatment of resonances through analytic continuation. These advances expand the range of realistic particle distributions that can be investigated using numerical dispersion solvers such as ALPS.

Multi-spacecraft measurements and heliophysics missions

Multi-point spacecraft observations provide new ways to distinguish spatial from temporal variability, characterize turbulent structure, and reconstruct gradients in evolving plasmas.

I am Deputy Principal Investigator of NASA’s HelioSwarm mission and Project Scientist for the SWEAP investigation aboard Parker Solar Probe. A central component of our work is developing and validating the measurement strategies and analysis methods needed to extract three-dimensional plasma physics from distributed spacecraft observations.

Highlighted publications

HelioSwarm: A Multipoint, Multiscale Mission to Characterize Turbulence
Klein et al. (2023), Space Science Reviews.

We describe the scientific motivation and measurement strategy of NASA’s nine-spacecraft HelioSwarm mission. By simultaneously sampling plasma fluctuations across multiple spatial separations, HelioSwarm will enable measurements of three-dimensional turbulent structure, intermittency, and multiscale energy transfer that are inaccessible to traditional single-spacecraft observations.

Wave-Telescope Analysis for Multipoint Observatories: Impact of Timing and Spatial Uncertainties
Klein, Broeren, Roberts & Schulz (2024), Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics.

We investigate how spacecraft timing and position uncertainties affect the reconstruction of spatial fluctuations using multipoint wave-telescope analysis. This work helps establish the measurement accuracy and analysis requirements needed to recover reliable three-dimensional information from future multispacecraft missions.
